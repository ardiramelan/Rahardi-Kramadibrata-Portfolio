LFP SOC curve and BMS settings

Typical rest voltage (OCV) curve of a LiFePO4 cell at 25 °C, with BMS setting limits and data measured on this pack. Move the SOC slider to read the voltage.

Analysis for BMS settings

Flat plateau: SOC cannot be read from voltage

Between about 20% and 90% SOC, cell voltage shifts by only about 0.15 V. That is why the BMS estimates SOC with coulomb counting and calibrates it when the pack is full. At 76.18 V (3.174 V average), the BMS showed 59% with a cycle count of 0, while the typical OCV curve points to about 19%. The BMS SOC reading only becomes reliable after one full charge.

Upper knee, where imbalance shows

Above 3.40 V the curve rises sharply, so capacity differences between cells show up immediately. The cell difference grew from 0.036 V at 76.18 V to 0.136 V at 82.43 V (cell 11: 3.539 V, cell 7: 3.405 V). This is why top balancing was done before assembly and BMS balancing is set to start at 3.40 V. At the 82.43 V reading, the balance feature was OFF, so this difference had not yet been trimmed.

Pack voltage limits

Nominal 24 x 3.2 V = 76.8 V. The absolute upper limit is 24 x 3.65 V = 87.6 V, while the charger only needs 84.0 to 85.2 V (3.50 to 3.55 V per cell) so the cells do not sit at the upper knee for long. The UVP lower limit of 2.80 V per cell equals 67.2 V.

Current and temperature

The BMS can handle 100 A, but current limits follow the cells: 30 Ah means 1C = 30 A. The 5.34 A discharge test (about 0.18C) only raised the MOS temperature to 31.6 °C and the cells to 30.5 to 30.7 °C. The balance lead resistances that could be read were uniform, 0.326 to 0.342 Ω.

Power system architecture

A single-line diagram simplified from the full SLD. Click any panel to see its calculation results.

Distribution hierarchy

20 kV utility, 2.5 MVA transformer, MSB, MDP, then downstream panels. Production, HVAC and utility, emergency, UPS, and BESS branches are separated for a clear protection and operating philosophy.

Load segregation

Dedicated panels for Machine A/B, FCU, exhaust fans, utility loads, MCC, UPS-backed systems, and BESS-supported loads. Fault isolation, maintenance, and future design development become simpler.

Power system analysis

Fault current and incident energy are treated as equipment-duty and safety inputs, not just drawing annotations. They drive interrupting ratings, protection review, arc-rated PPE requirements, and mitigation priorities.

Inspection method

Each system is checked with clear tools and criteria, so every finding can be traced to a number or photo evidence.

Electrical

Supply voltage, panels and MCBs, RCD protection, earthing, socket polarity, phase load balance, and visual condition. Reference: PUIL 2011 (SNI 0225:2011), voltage tolerance +5% / -10%, phase imbalance threshold 10%.

Quick performance screening for each split AC unit: 5 samples at 5-minute intervals. The main indicator is the Room-Supply ΔT: PASS if ΔT ≥ 3 °C, REVIEW if 0 to 3 °C, FAILED if ΔT < 0 °C.
